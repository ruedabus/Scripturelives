===============================================================
  MAV, MOONY & THE MIRACLE AT THE TOMB
  Faith Tails — Episode 7
  Scripture: John 11:25, 35, 43  |  Ages 7–12
===============================================================

KEY SCRIPTURE
  "Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life:
   he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live."
                                              — John 11:25 (KJV)

LESSON: No situation is too hopeless for God.
        Faith is believing before you can see.

CHARACTERS
  Mav    — a brave, curious golden dog
  Moony  — a wide-eyed, quick-thinking gray cat
  Jesus  — kind, calm, and certain — even when everyone else is afraid
  Martha — Lazarus' sister; faithful but heartbroken
  Mary   — Lazarus' sister; falls weeping at Jesus' feet
  The Disciples — nervous, loyal, wondering what Jesus will do next
  The Crowd — mourners who witness the impossible

SETTING: Judea, ancient Israel — the road to Bethany
         near Jerusalem, a rocky hillside tomb
===============================================================

— PAGE 1 —
TITLE: The Golden Portal Opens

It started, as it always did, without a warning.

Mav was chewing on a stick in the backyard. Moony was balanced
on the fence, watching a butterfly with great scientific interest.

Then — a shimmer. A hum. A swirl of golden light that spun up
from the ground like a tiny tornado.

The time portal.

Mav dropped his stick. Moony jumped off the fence.

"Here we go again," Moony said.

"YES!" barked Mav — and he leaped straight in.

ILLUSTRATION: Mav mid-leap into a glowing swirling golden portal
in a sunny backyard. Moony right behind him, ears back, eyes wide
but grinning. Warm golden light fills the scene.


— PAGE 2 —
TITLE: Ancient Judea

WHOOOOSH.

They tumbled out onto a dry, dusty road lined with olive trees.
The air smelled like warm stone and cedar wood.

Mav sneezed. Moony looked around, blinking.

The sky above was a deep blue. Rocky golden hills stretched in
every direction. In the distance, Mav could see the white walls
of a city shining in the sun.

"Where are we?" Moony whispered.

"I don't know," said Mav. "But it feels… ancient."

A group of men in robes were walking ahead of them down the road,
speaking in hushed, worried voices. Mav tilted his head. Something
was wrong.

ILLUSTRATION: Mav and Moony standing on a dusty dirt road in
ancient Judea — rocky hills, olive trees, Jerusalem visible in
the distant background. Warm sunlight. A group of robed figures
(the disciples) walking ahead on the road.


— PAGE 3 —
TITLE: Sad News

Mav and Moony padded quietly behind the group, staying low in the
tall dry grass along the road.

They could hear the men talking. Their voices were heavy.

"Lazarus is dead," one man said, shaking his head. "He was our
friend. He was a good man."

Another man sighed. "His sisters, Mary and Martha, must be
heartbroken."

"Four days," said a third. "He has been in the tomb four days now."

Mav felt a heaviness settle over him. Whoever Lazarus was, he was
gone — and the people who loved him were grieving.

Moony pressed close to Mav's side.

"What are they going to do?" Moony whispered.

Mav had no answer. But just then, the group parted — and Mav
saw Him.

ILLUSTRATION: Close-up of two disciples with sad, worried expressions,
one with hand over his heart. Mav and Moony are partially hidden
in the tall grass nearby, peering through, listening.


— PAGE 4 —
TITLE: Jesus

He walked at the center of the group, calm and unhurried.

The other men were worried. Some were afraid. But He was steady —
the way a mountain is steady when the wind blows around it.

Mav knew immediately who He was.

It was Jesus.

His eyes were kind, but they held something deep — like He already
knew exactly what was going to happen. Like He was not afraid of
death at all.

"Lord," one disciple said nervously, "the last time we went near
Jerusalem, the people tried to stone you. Is it safe to go back?"

Jesus turned to him with a quiet certainty.

"Our friend Lazarus sleepeth," Jesus said. "But I go, that I may
awake him out of sleep."

Mav and Moony exchanged a glance.

Sleep? Lazarus was dead — they had just heard it themselves.

ILLUSTRATION: Jesus walking calmly at the center of a group of
disciples on the dusty road. He looks serene and certain. The
disciples around him look anxious and uncertain. Warm light
radiates gently around Jesus. Mav and Moony are small figures in
the background watching.


— PAGE 5 —
TITLE: The Journey Begins

"Let us go to him," Jesus said simply.

And just like that, the group turned and headed south toward
Bethany.

One disciple — a man named Thomas — crossed his arms and muttered
to the others, "Well… let us also go, that we may die with him."

He was scared. They were all scared. But they followed anyway.

Mav watched this and felt something stir in his chest.

They didn't know what was going to happen. They were walking
toward danger, toward grief, toward a sealed stone tomb. And yet
they went — because Jesus said go.

Moony tugged on Mav's ear. "Are we going too?"

Mav took one look at Jesus, already walking steadily down the road.

"We're going," Mav said.

And they followed.

ILLUSTRATION: The group walking down a winding road through
rocky Judean hills. Jesus leads at the front. Disciples follow
in a cluster, some looking nervous. Mav and Moony trot along
at the back of the group, tails up, determined.


— PAGE 6 —
TITLE: The Road to Bethany

The road wound through sun-baked hills and rocky fields.
Date palms swayed in the warm breeze. Goats watched from
the hillside as the group passed.

Mav walked close to Jesus. He had been on adventures before —
but never one like this. There was a weight in the air, like the
whole earth was holding its breath.

Moony was uncharacteristically quiet.

"Jesus doesn't seem worried," Mav whispered.

"No," Moony said. "He seems like He already knows how the
story ends."

Mav thought about that. To walk toward something scary
without fear — not because it wasn't real, but because
you already knew the ending.

Was that what faith was?

The city of Bethany appeared ahead — a small village of
flat-roofed homes tucked into the hills just east of Jerusalem.

ILLUSTRATION: The road curving through beautiful ancient Judean
countryside — golden hills, date palms, a few goats. The small
village of Bethany visible ahead. Mav and Moony walking side by
side, looking thoughtful. Beautiful warm afternoon light.


— PAGE 7 —
TITLE: Martha Comes Running

Before they even reached the village, a woman came running
down the road toward them.

Her eyes were red from crying. Her face was tired from grief.
But when she saw Jesus, something lit up in her — hope and
heartbreak all at once.

It was Martha, Lazarus' sister.

She stopped in front of Jesus, breathing hard.

"Lord," she said, her voice breaking, "if thou hadst been here,
my brother had not died."

Mav felt the weight of those words. She wasn't angry — she
believed in Jesus. But she was hurting. She had watched her
brother die, and Jesus had not come in time.

Or so it seemed.

"But I know," Martha continued, lifting her eyes, "that even
now, whatsoever thou wilt ask of God, God will give it thee."

Even in her grief, she still trusted Him.

ILLUSTRATION: Martha running down the dusty road toward Jesus,
tears on her face, robes flowing. She stops before Him, hands
clasped. Jesus looks at her with great compassion. The disciples
stand back respectfully. Mav and Moony watch from nearby, moved.


— PAGE 8 —
TITLE: I AM the Resurrection

Jesus looked at Martha with steady, loving eyes.

"Thy brother shall rise again," He said.

Martha nodded sadly. "I know that he shall rise again in the
resurrection at the last day."

She thought He was talking about someday — about the end of all
things. But Jesus was about to say something that no one had
ever said before.

He drew a slow breath, and His voice was clear and certain.

"I am the resurrection, and the life."

Mav's ears perked straight up.

"He that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live.
And whosoever liveth and believeth in me shall never die."

He looked at Martha gently.

"Believest thou this?"

Martha looked back at Him — and through her tears, she said yes.

ILLUSTRATION: Jesus speaking to Martha. His expression is warm
but deeply certain — like someone who knows a great secret.
Martha is looking up at him, tear-streaked but beginning to hope.
Soft golden light surrounds them. Mav's ears are perked forward,
alert. Moony's eyes are wide.


— PAGE 9 —
TITLE: Mary Weeps

Martha ran back to the village to find her sister Mary.

"The Master is here," she whispered. "And he calleth for thee."

Mary rose quickly and ran to Jesus. When she reached Him,
she fell at His feet — weeping.

"Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died."

The crowd of mourners who had come to comfort the family
followed behind her, and when they saw her weeping — they wept
too. The whole hillside filled with the sound of grief.

Mav had seen sadness before. But not like this. This was the
sound of a world that knew death too well — that had seen too
much loss, too much pain, too many goodbyes.

He looked up at Jesus.

And what he saw surprised him.

ILLUSTRATION: Mary fallen at Jesus' feet, weeping. A crowd of
mourners behind her, many crying. The landscape of rocky Judean
hills behind them. Mav looking up at Jesus, expression curious
and moved. Moony close beside Mav.


— PAGE 10 —
TITLE: Jesus Wept

Jesus saw Mary weeping. He saw the crowd weeping. He felt the
weight of their grief pressing in on all sides.

And Jesus — the Son of God, the Creator of the universe, the One
who already knew exactly what He was about to do — felt it too.

He was deeply moved.

His eyes filled with tears.

And then the shortest verse in all of the Bible happened:

"Jesus wept."

Just two words. But Mav felt them like a thunder crack in his chest.

Jesus didn't stand apart from their pain. He entered it.
He sat in it. He cried right along with them.

"Look how he loved him," someone in the crowd said softly.

Moony's voice came out in a whisper. "He's… crying."

"Yeah," Mav said quietly. "He is."

And somehow, that made everything feel different.

ILLUSTRATION: A close, intimate illustration of Jesus with tears
on his face. Not dramatic — quiet and tender. His eyes are full
of compassion. The crowd around Him is weeping. A shaft of warm
light falls on Him. Mav watches with wide, glistening eyes.


— PAGE 11 —
TITLE: The Tomb

Jesus wiped His eyes. He straightened. And something shifted
in the air — like a storm gathering, like the moment before
a great wave.

"Where have ye laid him?" He asked.

"Lord, come and see," they said.

They led Jesus — and the crowd, and the disciples, and two
small animals who had traveled across time to be there — to
the edge of a hillside.

There it was.

A cave carved into the rock, sealed shut by a massive round
stone. Heavy. Final. Cold.

Four days. Lazarus had been in there four days.

Mav swallowed hard. Even he could smell the weight of death
on the stone.

Moony pressed against his side. "Mav… what is He going to do?"

"I don't know," Mav said. "But I think we're about to find out."

ILLUSTRATION: The crowd standing before the rock-hewn tomb —
a cave entrance sealed with a large stone rolled in front.
Rocky hillside setting, dry ground, some people in the crowd
looking uneasy. Jesus stands before it, calm and purposeful.
Mav and Moony in the foreground, staring at the tomb.


— PAGE 12 —
TITLE: Roll Away the Stone

Jesus turned to the people standing nearest the tomb.

"Take ye away the stone," He said.

A murmur ran through the crowd. People shifted uncomfortably.

Martha stepped forward, wringing her hands. "Lord, by this time
he stinketh: for he hath been dead four days."

She wasn't wrong. Four days was too long. There was no
coming back from four days. Everyone knew that.

Everyone except One.

Jesus turned to her — the same steady, certain expression
He had worn since the moment they left the road.

"Said I not unto thee, that, if thou wouldest believe,
thou shouldest see the glory of God?"

Martha was still for a moment.

Then she stepped back.

And the men moved toward the stone.

ILLUSTRATION: Several men straining against a massive round stone,
beginning to roll it away from the tomb entrance. Martha stands
nearby, hands clasped, expression a mix of fear and hope.
Jesus stands steady. The crowd watches, breath held.


— PAGE 13 —
TITLE: Jesus Prays

The stone groaned and rolled.

Dust rose from the ground. The tomb entrance yawned open —
dark and still.

The crowd went completely silent.

Jesus lifted His face to the sky. His eyes were open. His voice
was calm and clear — not a desperate plea, but a confident
conversation with Someone He knew completely.

"Father, I thank thee that thou hast heard me."

Mav had heard prayers before — quick ones, nervous ones,
hopeful ones. This prayer was different. It was certain.
Like thanking someone for a gift they had already given,
before you had even opened it.

"And I knew that thou hearest me always: but because of the
people which stand by I said it, that they may believe
that thou hast sent me."

He was praying for the crowd. For Martha. For Mary.
For every person watching.

For Mav. For Moony.

For everyone who would ever need to believe.

ILLUSTRATION: Jesus looking upward with His eyes open, praying.
Light seems to descend around Him. The open tomb is behind Him.
The crowd watches in hushed stillness. Mav and Moony are on
their knees (front paws down), heads bowed.


— PAGE 14 —
TITLE: The Loudest Voice

The prayer ended.

Silence.

Not a single person moved. Not a single person breathed.

Mav's heart hammered.

Then Jesus turned toward the tomb. He planted His feet in the
dry dirt. He pulled in a breath.

And in a voice that seemed to fill the entire valley —

"LAZARUS — COME FORTH!"

The words rang off the rocky hillside and echoed out across
the land. The sound of it made Mav's whole body tremble.

It wasn't a shout of desperation. It wasn't a cry of hope.

It was a command.

The voice of the One who had spoken the stars into existence,
who had formed the mountains with His breath —

telling death itself to give something back.

And everything went still.

ILLUSTRATION: Jesus at the tomb entrance, one arm outstretched
toward the dark opening, voice powerful and commanding. His
robes move as if in a wind, light surrounding Him. The crowd
recoils slightly from the power of the moment. Mav and Moony
stare at the dark tomb opening, wide-eyed, waiting.


— PAGE 15 —
TITLE: He Comes Out

One second. Two. Three.

And then —

Movement.

From inside the dark tomb, something stirred.

A shuffling sound. Slow. Deliberate.

And then, from the darkness, a figure appeared — wrapped head
to toe in strips of white linen, his face covered in a burial
cloth, moving in small, careful steps toward the light.

Lazarus.

The crowd erupted.

Screaming. Sobbing. People grabbing each other. Someone
fainted. A woman dropped to her knees right where she stood.

Mav let out a bark so loud that three disciples jumped.

Moony stood completely frozen, mouth open, not making a sound.

He was dead. He was dead for four days.

And now he was walking.

ILLUSTRATION: Lazarus emerging from the dark tomb entrance,
fully wrapped in white linen grave clothes, blinking in the
bright sunlight. The crowd around him is in pure shock — hands
over mouths, tears streaming, arms reaching out. The scene is
electric with awe. Mav is mid-bark, tail going crazy.


— PAGE 16 —
TITLE: Loose Him and Let Him Go

"Loose him," Jesus said, smiling now, "and let him go."

The crowd surged forward. Hands carefully, gently began
unwrapping the linen from Lazarus' arms, his face, his feet.

And there he was — blinking in the bright afternoon sun,
looking confused and alive and completely, perfectly whole.

Martha and Mary ran to him. They threw their arms around
their brother, laughing and crying at the same time — the kind
of crying that only happens when something impossible
turns out to be real.

People were falling to their knees. Many of them — people who
had come only to mourn — believed in Jesus right there on
that hillside.

Because what else could you do?

What argument could you make against a man who had just walked
out of his own tomb?

ILLUSTRATION: Martha and Mary embracing a fully unwrapped,
living Lazarus — huge smiles through tears. Lazarus looks
joyful and bewildered. The crowd around them is celebrating,
many kneeling in worship. Jesus watches with deep joy.


— PAGE 17 —
TITLE: Mav Couldn't Stop Wagging

Mav's tail had not stopped moving for five full minutes.

He ran in a circle. He barked at the sky. He jumped up and
put his paws on Moony's shoulders (which Moony did not
appreciate at all).

"HE'S ALIVE, MOONY! LAZARUS IS ALIVE!"

"I SAW IT," Moony said. "I was right here! You can stop
jumping on me now!"

But even Moony — calm, cool, scientific Moony — had tears
streaming down his gray face.

They had traveled through time before. They had seen storms
calmed, lions tamed, giants fall.

But they had never seen death reversed.

They had just watched the impossible happen.

And they knew — they would never forget it.

ILLUSTRATION: Mav jumping joyfully in the air, tail blurred
from wagging. Moony standing nearby, crying and trying to
look dignified about it. The celebration continues in the
background. A joyful, light scene — color and movement.


— PAGE 18 —
TITLE: Only Believe

As the celebration swirled around them, Mav felt a presence
beside him.

He turned.

Jesus was standing there — looking down at the two of them
with a warm, quiet smile. As if He had known all along that
they were there. As if He had been glad about it.

"You were afraid," He said — not as a question.

Mav thought about it honestly. "Yes," he admitted. "When
I saw the tomb… I thought it was over."

Jesus nodded slowly.

"That is what it looked like," He said. "But fear looks at
what is seen. Faith looks at what is promised." He paused.
"I told Martha — if she would believe, she would see the
glory of God."

He looked from Mav to Moony.

"The glory of God is not found after the miracle. It is found
in the believing — before the stone is rolled away."

ILLUSTRATION: Jesus kneeling down to Mav and Moony's level,
face to face with them. His expression is warm and certain.
Mav sits, ears forward, taking it all in. Moony is wide-eyed
and still. Behind them, the crowd celebrates Lazarus' return.


— PAGE 19 —
TITLE: Nothing Is Too Hard

Moony looked up at Jesus.

"But what about the people who pray and don't see a miracle?"
Moony asked quietly. "What about the ones who believe but
their person doesn't come back?"

Jesus was quiet for a moment. His eyes were gentle.

"I wept with them too," He said. "Even knowing what was coming
— I sat in their grief. I did not stand above it. I entered it."

He placed a hand gently on Moony's head.

"There are miracles you will see — and miracles you will
not see yet. But this is what is true in both: I am with you.
I have not forgotten you. And nothing — not sickness, not
loss, not even death — is the final word when I am speaking."

He smiled.

"Trust Me. Even before the stone moves."

ILLUSTRATION: Jesus with one hand on Moony's small gray head,
looking at them both with deep compassion and love. Warm
afternoon light. The scene is quiet and intimate — just three
figures on a hillside while the world celebrates behind them.


— PAGE 20 —
TITLE: Back Home — Changed

The golden portal hummed open behind them.

Mav looked at it. Then he looked back at Bethany — at Lazarus
standing in the sunlight with his family, at the people who
had come to mourn and were leaving as believers.

Then he looked at Jesus one more time.

Jesus raised His hand. A simple goodbye.

Mav and Moony stepped through the light.

—

They tumbled back into the backyard. Same fence. Same butterfly.
Same warm afternoon. Like no time had passed at all.

But something was different. Something inside them had shifted.

Mav sat down in the grass and looked at the sky.

He thought about what Jesus said.
Trust Me. Even before the stone moves.

He thought about Martha. About Mary weeping. About the crowd
who had no hope left — until they had more hope than they knew
what to do with.

He thought about those two words.

Jesus wept.

And then — just as powerfully — those three words.

LAZARUS, COME FORTH.

Mav smiled.

There is nothing too hard for God. Nothing too broken. Nothing
too hopeless. No tomb too sealed. No night too dark.

If you believe — you will see the glory of God.

ILLUSTRATION: Mav and Moony back in their familiar backyard,
sitting together in the grass. The golden portal fades behind
them. They are both looking up at a bright, hopeful sky.
The scene feels peaceful and full of wonder. Warm golden light.

The Story Behind the Story

The miracle of Lazarus is found in John 11 in the Bible.
Jesus knew Lazarus was sick, but He waited — not because
He didn't care, but because He had a greater plan.

When Jesus arrived four days after Lazarus had died, everyone
thought it was too late. But Jesus showed them that nothing is
too late for God.

Two of the most powerful moments in this story:
• John 11:35 — "Jesus wept." The shortest verse in the Bible.
  It tells us that Jesus enters our grief — He doesn't ignore it.
• John 11:43 — "Lazarus, come forth!" The voice that spoke
  creation into existence called one man back from death.

Key Verse to Remember:
"I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me,
though he were dead, yet shall he live." — John 11:25 (KJV)

Talk About It:
• Why did Jesus wait before going to see Lazarus?
• Why do you think Jesus cried, even though He knew what was
  about to happen?
• What is something in your life where you need to trust God
  before you see the answer?
